## 7. TEMPORAL ADMISSION SEMANTICS IMPLEMENTED

Implemented temporal admission behavior preserves the accepted T-window semantics:

- caller supplies aware-UTC state_reference_time T
- applicable fields use a finite (T - W, T] window
- observation_time, ingestion_time, event_time, and processing_time remain distinct
- null observation_time remains null
- receipt time MUST NOT substitute for missing source time
- evidence received after T cannot support as-of current state
- evidence known to have been canonicalized after T cannot support that as-of state
- unknown processing time does not prove historical consumption
- future/inconsistent clocks are excluded rather than clamped fresh
- receipt-only evidence may describe what Guardian received but does NOT establish physical source measurement age
- stale-only evidence cannot populate a current-value field
- stale evidence may remain explicit last-known/context evidence
- missing, stale, and source failure remain distinct
- receipt proximity is not treated as physical simultaneity
- shared acquisition ID is not treated as physical simultaneity

S3 preserves original M3.1 quality/freshness semantics and adds its own admission assessment without rewriting M3.1 semantics.

## 8. SUBJECT / IDENTITY-RESOLUTION SEMANTICS IMPLEMENTED

Implemented identity resolution behavior:

- resolves subject/workload/algorithm using supplied immutable mapping snapshots
- preserves fixed scoped identities or explicit unknowns
- does not perform registry discovery or mutation
- does not use row-position identity
- does not guess GPU-to-workload mapping
- does not infer identity from alias-based network heuristics
- unknown identity remains unknown and is recorded as such
- subject/workload/algorithm mismatch is surfaced rather than silently unified

Identity resolution is deterministic given the same canonical evidence and the same immutable mapping snapshot.

## 9. CONTINUITY SEMANTICS IMPLEMENTED

Implemented continuity behavior:

- derives analytical evidence segments and break evidence deterministically
- preserves segment provenance including source, source instance, signal, subject, workload/algorithm, ordering basis, segment anchor, boundary evidence, proven incarnation if any, and segmentation method/version
- breaks a segment on applicable conditions including explicit incarnation change, counter decrease/reset, recorded discontinuity, subject change, workload change, algorithm change, counter-semantics change, excessive configured gap, unresolved ordering conflict, and unresolved identity conflict
- does NOT calculate delta/rate across a segment boundary
- does NOT clamp a negative delta into continuity
- does NOT assume rollover
- does NOT infer restart from uptime decrease
- does NOT infer restart from counter decrease
- does NOT derive physical share-production rate from receipt-only time
- does NOT use M3.1 process-local continuity state as deterministic replay authority
- unknown process incarnation remains unknown

## 10. CONFLICT SEMANTICS IMPLEMENTED

Implemented conflict detection behavior:

- preserves every participant in StateConflict records
- supports accepted conflict kinds:
  - VALUE_DISAGREEMENT
  - IDENTITY_MISMATCH
  - TEMPORAL_MISALIGNMENT
  - UNIT_OR_SEMANTIC_MISMATCH
- records conflict ID, affected field, conflict kind, all participant evidence references, comparison contract ID/version, comparison basis, resolution status/reason, and optional explicit resolution rule/version where applicable
- compares evidence only when subject, quantity, unit, and effective interval are demonstrably compatible
- initially implements NO general cross-source winner
- rejects implicit latest-wins, preferred-source, max/min, averaging, majority vote, and provider-ranking policies
- preserves all conflict participants and all agreement participants
- records comparable agreement provenance when sources agree
- orders outputs deterministically

Accepted conflict behavior for unresolved comparable eligible conflict:

- current field consequence is UNKNOWN downstream
- positive integrity/identity violation may become INVALID downstream
- S3 does not implement S4 field assessment or assembly behavior

## 11. DETERMINISTIC IDENTITY / REPLAY BEHAVIOR

Implemented deterministic identity behavior:

- conflict IDs are UUIDs, but random UUIDs are forbidden in deterministic semantic content
- conflict and segment identities are therefore derived deterministically
- the concrete deterministic derivation mechanism is an implementation-level, versioned choice
- the derivation must be deterministic, include all semantically identity-relevant inputs, avoid collisions within supported semantics, be stable for identical inputs, and change when identity-relevant semantic inputs change
- deterministic results hold under shuffled input order where ordering semantics permit
- identical admissible inputs produce identical S3 outputs without reliance on hidden wall clock, random IDs in the semantic core, mutable registry lookups, or unordered iteration affecting output
- S3 outputs are pure/reconstructible semantic intermediates suitable for later preservation by S6

## 13. DISCOVERED AND CORRECTED CONFLICT DEFECT

During S3-D verification, a real defect was discovered and corrected in the conflict envelope.

### Original defect

The original conflict envelope returned `not_compared` too early for certain same-source valued candidates and could suppress unit/value-kind semantic mismatches.

### Corrected behavior

The corrected behavior surfaces demonstrably comparable unit/value-kind mismatches as:

- UNIT_OR_SEMANTIC_MISMATCH

The fix tightened the early-return condition so that unit/value-kind mismatches are surfaced as conflicts whenever they are demonstrably comparable, while still preserving the intended single-candidate and insufficient-source-diversity not-compared behaviors.

This is recorded as a corrected defect, not as a remaining defect.
